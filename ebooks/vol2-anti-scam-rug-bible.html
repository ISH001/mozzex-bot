Chapter 01 • The Anti-Scam & Rug Bible

The Anatomy of an On-Chain Rug Pull

In the decentralized memecoin trenches, more than 90% of newly minted tokens are created with the express intention of extracting liquidity from retail buyers. To protect your hard-earned capital, you must understand the four primary mechanisms malicious developers use to scam you:

The Four Common Rug Models

  • The Classic Liquidity Pull: The developer launches a token, pairs it with 5 to 20 SOL in a liquidity pool, lets buyers pump the price, and then withdraws the SOL from the pool, making the token un-tradable.
  • The Mint Dump: The developer retains "Mint Authority" on the smart contract. Once buyers push the market cap to $50k or $100k, the dev mints 1,000,000,000 fresh tokens out of thin air and dumps them onto the market.
  • The Freeze Honeypot: The developer leaves "Freeze Authority" active. When retail users buy the token, the smart contract freezes their wallet, preventing them from selling while the dev dumps their own bag.
  • The Coordinated Team Dump: The dev creates 15 separate fresh wallets before launch and distributes 50% of the token supply among them. As soon as the coin launches, these clustered wallets dump simultaneously.

The 60-Second Contract Audit Checklist

Before putting a single dollar into any new token, open RugCheck.xyz or Axiom and verify these four non-negotiable security flags:

Security Check Safe Status (Green Flag) Dangerous Status (Red Flag - DO NOT BUY)
Mint Authority Disabled / Revoked Enabled (Dev can print unlimited tokens)
Freeze Authority Disabled / Revoked Enabled (Dev can freeze your wallet)
Liquidity (LP) 100% Burned or Locked Unlocked (Dev can pull liquidity anytime)
Top 10 Holders Less than 15% - 20% total Greater than 30% (Severe dump risk)
🛡️ The Absolute Zero-Tolerance Rule If Mint Authority or Freeze Authority is NOT revoked, DO NOT BUY. It does not matter how good the meme looks, how fast it is pumping, or what influencers are tweeting. Unrevoked authority is an immediate pass.

Detecting Dev Bundles & Insider Clusters

Smart scammers no longer keep 40% of the token supply in their main developer wallet. They use automated bundling software (like Jito bundles) to distribute supply across 10 to 30 fresh burner wallets in the exact same block as the token launch.

How to Spot Bundled Wallets Using BubbleMaps

Use BubbleMaps or Solscan to visualize the wallet relationships:

  • Identical Funding Source: Look at the SOL funding source for the top 10 buyer wallets. If 8 of the top 10 wallets were funded by the exact same centralized exchange deposit or parent wallet within 10 minutes of launch, it is a single individual controlling the supply.
  • Simultaneous First-Block Buys: In block transactions, check the first 5 transactions. If multiple wallets bought identical percentages with identical gas fees within the same second, they are bundled snipers controlled by the dev team.
⚠️ The Fake Volume Trap Developers often run wash-trading volume bots that buy and sell to themselves repeatedly to force their coin onto the "DexScreener Trending" list. Always look at the ratio between 24-hour volume and total holders. If volume is $500,000 but there are only 42 holders, 95% of the volume is fake wash trading.

Social Engineering & Fake Community Forensics

A shiny website and a Telegram group with 5,000 members does not mean a project is legitimate. Scammers spend $50 to buy 10,000 fake Telegram members and automated AI chat bots that repeat messages like "To the moon!", "LFG!", and "Dev is based!".

How to Audit Telegram & Twitter / X in 3 Minutes

  • Telegram Chat Velocity Test: Join the Telegram. Ask a specific, critical question: "Hey dev, what is your LP lock TX signature and why is top holder holding 8%?" If you are instantly muted or banned, it is a scam.
  • Twitter Account Age & History: Inspect the Twitter handle. Check if the handle was created 3 days ago, or if it was an old renamed account that previously posted in a completely different language.
  • Engagement-to-View Ratio: A tweet with 500 likes but only 3 comments and 600 views is artificially boosted with cheap bot engagement. Genuine viral communities have organic replies and community-created memes.

Advanced Traps: Taxes, Blacklists & Fake Migrations

As retail traders have become smarter, scam developers have developed more sophisticated, sneaky traps:

1. Dynamic Transfer Fees (Hidden Sell Taxes)

Token extensions on Solana allow creators to implement transfer fees. A coin might show 0% tax on buy, but when you attempt to sell, the contract deducts a 99% transfer tax, leaving you with pennies. Always verify token extensions on Solscan.

2. Fake Migration & Airdrop Claims

When a coin begins dumping, the developer often announces in Telegram: "We are migrating to V2! Click this link to connect your wallet and claim your V2 airdrop!" This link is a wallet drainer script that will steal every token and SOL in your wallet. Never connect your wallet to claim unexpected "airdrop migrations".

Emergency Action Plan & Post-Rug Recovery

If you realize you are holding a coin where the developer is actively dumping or preparing to rug, every single second counts:

Emergency Evacuation Protocol:
1. Do not try to wait for a bounce. A rug pull bounce is almost always an illusion.
2. Set slippage to 15% - 25% immediately to force your sell transaction through ahead of remaining liquidity drain.
3. Submit the transaction with maximum priority gas. Getting back 50% or 60% of your capital is 100x better than watching it hit $0.00.
4. Disconnect your burner wallet from the dApp using your wallet settings.
5. Revoke any pending approvals on Solana using revoke.cash or Solscan token approvals.

Remember: In trading, surviving bad situations with partial capital preserves your ability to fight another day. Never let stubborn pride turn a 30% loss into a 100% total loss.
